<u>Similarities:</u>
- They both believe that reason is essential to virtue, and that we should develop our capacity for rational thought.  Neither philosopher thinks that simply following your immediate feelings or accepting your first impression is enough to live well.  We must examine our thinking
- Mistakes and lapses in judgement can prevent us from living well
	- **Epictetus** believes that we become disturbed when we judge external events to be inherently terrible or essential to our happiness
	- **Astell** believes that we fall into error when we accept unclear ideas, allow biases to influence us, or claim more than our evidence supports
- Both believe that we should not entirely depend on external approval from others to determine our well-being.  However, Epictetus believes this because it's outside of our control, and thus we should not let them affect out tranquility.  Whereas Estell believes this more so for the fact that women shouldn't be seeking the approval of men as a way to measure their self worth
- They both distinguish reality from our judgements about reality- take the golden mountain for example

<u>Differences:</u>
- They disagree on what the meaning of happiness is, Epictetus thinks happiness comes from peace and mental tranquility whereas Estell believes it comes from self esteem and the pursuit of knowledge, and by fulfilling our moral duties to God
- When it comes to reason, Epictetus believes that we should assess what's within our power, correct our judgements, and then top demanding that the world conform to our wishes.  However with Astell, she believes that we should examine the clarity and truth of ideas, organize arguments, resist prejudice, and limit our beliefs to what we can justify
- Epictetus is primarily concerned with how we attach our happiness to external factors and how we judge uncontrollable events incorrectly.  Astell is more concerned with how we make reasoning errors, and how women face social expectations that discourage their rational and intellectual development
- When it comes to got, Astell is very religious and thus believes we owe a moral duty to god, and that developing and using reason is how we fulfil that moral duty.  Epictetus thinks that the universe is rationally and divinely ordered, and that we should understand our place within that order and accept what we cannot control

| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| <u>**External Influences**</u>    | Wealth, status, other people's opinions, etc.                                    | Social expectations, particularly those imposed on women, can obstruct intellectual and moral development |
| <u>**Main Goal**</u>              | Achieving happiness through mental tranquility and inner freedom                 | Achieving happiness through self esteem and intellectual growth, while fulfilling moral duty to god       |
| <u>**Other peoples opinions**</u> | This is an external factor outside of your control; do not let it bring you down | Don't measure your worth by men's opinions, beauty standards, or marital status                           |
| <u>**Practical Method**</u>       | Distinguish what is within your control from what isn't                          | Examine ideas carefully, remove irrelevant information, and judge only as far as the evidence allows      |
| <u>**Religion & Universe**</u>    | A rational, divinely ordered universe whose events we should learn to accept     | God gave humans reason, and we have a duty to develop and use it properly                                 |
| <u>**Role of Reason**</u>         | Help us judge events correctly and govern our responses                          | Helps us identify truth, correct errors, and avoid false beliefs                                          |
| <u>**Source of Happiness**</u>    | Mental tranquility and freedom from disturbance                                  | Self-esteem, intellectual self-improvement, and living according to moral duty                            |
| <u>**Source of Problems**</u>     | Mistaken judgements about events outside of our control                          | Errors in reasoning, prejudice, misinformation, and unjustified beliefs                                   |
| <u>**Virtue**</u>                 | Living rationally and accepting what is outside of our control                   | Developing our rational abilities and reasoning correctly                                                 |
| <u>**What we should control**</u> | Our judgements, choices, and reponses                                            | Our reasoning, judgments, and willingness to accept or reject claims                                      |
- <u>Virtue:</u> Excellence of character expressed through rational choices
- <u>Ataraxia:</u> Freedom from disturbance; mental tranquility
- <u>Apatheia:</u> Freedom from irrational or destructive passions, rather than an inability to feel anything
- <u>Astell's Six Rules of Reasoning:</u>
	- Rule 1: "Acquaint our selves thoroughly with the state of the question, have a distinct notion of our subject"
		- Avoid irrelevant ideas
	- Rule 2: "Cut out all needless ideas and whatever has not a connection to the matter under consideration"
		- Clear our minds of fuzzy ideas
	- Rule 3: Order our reasoning, from the simplest to the most complex ideas
	- Rule 4: Don't leave any part of the question unexamined- everything is up for scrutiny
	- Rule 5: Keep the subject directly in our eye- do not veer of onto other subjects
	- Rule 6: To judge no further than we perceive, and not to take any thing for truth which we do not evidently know to be so
		- For example, don't assume alien civilizations are all technologically advanced and will come to invade us arriving in ships
- Astell's ideal of independence is not merely refusing to care what anyone thinks. It involves cultivating the ability to think clearly, evaluate beliefs for oneself, and pursue moral and intellectual excellence.
