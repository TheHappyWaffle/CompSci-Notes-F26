**

- **<u>Virtue: </u>**A habit or character trait that enables individuals to live a good life.
- **<u>Happiness:</u>** A state of being that results from living a virtuous life.
- **<u>Human Function:</u>** The unique activities and characteristics that define human beings.
- **<u>Practical Wisdom:</u>** The ability to make decisions and act in accordance with reason and virtue.

- No one wants to live a life without meaning, purpose, or satisfaction.  But how do we go about fulfilling each of these?

- Lot's of things make life feel meaningful, like money, friends, a car, etc.  But even with all of these we can still feel unhappy or unfulfilled, desirous of something more.  But what...?
	- All of these things are aimed at something else, they're instruments for some further good, but what is the end point? You can use them to help you achieve happiness, but they will never be the source of it

- Aristotle thinks we can make mistakes, we can aim at things that seem like happiness but ultimately aren't.  But it will always feel as though we are chasing something but never quite achieving it

- "But I'm happy when I get a promotion at work or go out with friends!" Yes, but Aristotle defines happiness as something more than a temporary or fleeting sense of joy

- For Aristotle, a happy life requires activity in accordance with reason- This is excellence, or virtue
	- This is the expression of our function.  This life is a flourishing one; it is the fulfilment of your human nature, your unique capacities as a human being

- The function of a human being is to act in a rational manner, to be reflective and thoughtful, and to consider how best to act in any given situation

- The happy life (according to Aristotle) is constantly devoted to rational activity and performing in a way that expresses our human capacities
	- For Aristotle, happiness is the way we do things, not *just* having things

- What does a virtuous life look like?
	- <u>Intellectual virtues</u> such as wisdom and understanding
		- Intellectual virtues require learning and time
	- <u>Moral virtues</u> such as courage, self control, generosity, etc.
		- Moral virtues require practice and habituation
		- "Habit brings this ability to completion and fulfilment" (76)
	- All of the above are character traits, and character says something about the way we live our lives

- But what are virtues?
	- Someone who might be kind, generous, courageous, patient, caring, etc.
		- *These* are known as <u>virtues</u>
	- Someone who is likely not mean spirited, arrogant, selfish, uncaring, or unempathetic
		- *These* are known as *vices*

- A good or happy life requires that we aim to develop the virtues and to avoid developing vices, and to do so in a deliberate and considered way

- <u>Happiness</u> is not just how we feel in the moment, but how we live and the goals we aim at
	- And the more we commit to this end and practice these virtues, the closer we come to excellence and flourishing
	- "We become just by the practice of just actions, self controlled by exercising self-control, and courageous by performing acts of courage" (77)
	- This requires deliberate & rational action
		- Virtues requires us to know what we're doing, we can't do a 'good' action thoughtlessly or accidentally.  You have to know what you're doing and that it's the right thing to do
		- We cannot go about doing these acts simply for praise or approval or some other ulterior motive, it has to be for it's own sake
		- It's never just what you do, it's how and why you do it
		- The virtues have to be expressed in moderation, don't just give everything you have to charity.  This is otherwise known as the <u>Goldilocks Formula</u>
		- "to experience all of this at the right time, toward the right objects, toward the right people, for the right reason, and in the right manner- that is the median and the best course, the course that is a mark of virtue" (82)
		- So virtue is a tendency to act in a certain way in any given situation in a balanced and appropriate manner
