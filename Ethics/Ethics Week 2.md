- You shouldn't just follow rules or laws because you have to, you should want to

- Aristotle doesn't think people who are selfish or judgmental or unvirtuous can truly be happy or esteemed of them selves

- In order to be truly happy and esteemed of your self, you must have virtues and be able to express them in the most appropriate way at all times.  A.K.A. The [[Goldilocks Formula]].  And the result will be a life that is morally excellent and happy

- Can I bee too courageous? too modest?
	- Courage is a virtue, but cowardice and fear is the corresponding vice.  However too much courage, and you become reckless
	- You can have self-control rather than be insensitive, but too much self control and you over indulge yourself

- In a road rage incident, you might respond by cutting them off, flipping them the bird, or yelling at them, but after the incident we might feel bad or shameful about it.  Aristotle wants us to look like how we ought to act (assuming we want to be a good person).  So Aristotle says:
	- "In regard to anger, there exists an excess, a deficiency, and a mean...we might  
		call the mean gentleness..of the extremes, let the man who exceeds be called  
		short tempered and his vice a short temper, and the deficient man apathetic  
		and his vice apathy” (84)
	- So in the road rage incident, don't just brush it off and pretend it didn't happen, that's sloth or apathy.  Express your anger but do not escalate or make the situation worse.  Being a virtuous person means you know how to do it and how to do it well

- Work on the urge to over react or under react in situations.  Practice makes perfect, through trial and error you can become a virtuous person
