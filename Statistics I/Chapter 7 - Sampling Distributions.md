- <u>Sampling Distribution:</u> The probability distribution of a statistic.  For example, if we repeatedly sampled from the population, calculated the statistic for each sample, and plotted the histogram of values, that histogram would resemble the sampling distribution of the statistic
	- Let $\bar{X}$ be the sample mean of $n$ independent observations, it can be calculated with: $$\bar{X} = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \dots + X_n}{n}$$where $X_n$ is each independently drawn observation with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $σ$, and $σ$ can be calculated with: $$σ_{\bar{x}} = \frac{σ}{\sqrt{n}}$$$
- If $X$ is normally distributed with $\mu$ and $σ$ then $$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{σ}$$We can use this to find the probabilities of a single observation
- If $X$ is normally distributed with mean $\mu_{\bar{X}}$ and $σ_{\bar{x}} = \frac{σ}{\sqrt{n}}$ then $$Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_\bar{X}}{σ_\bar{X}} = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{σ / \sqrt{n}}$$has the standard normal distribution.  We can use this to find probabilities relating to the mean of $n$ observations
- <u>Central Limit Theorum:</u> If we are sampling from a distribution that is not normal, then the distribution of $X$ gets closer to normal as the sample size increases, and if the sample size is large enough the sampling distribution of $X$ will be approximately normal.  $X$ can be considered approximately normal when $n > 30$
- <u>Standard Error:</u> The estimate of standard deviation of a statistics sampling distribution.  It measures how much the value of the stat would vary from sample to sample
