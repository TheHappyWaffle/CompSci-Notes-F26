- <u>Sequences:</u> Sequences can be infinite or finite, usually defined by an explicit formula which shows how each term $a_n$ depends on it's index $n$
- <u>Arithmetic Progression:</u> A sequence of the form: $a+d, a+2d, a+3d, a+4d,$ etc. where a and d (the common difference) are real numbers
- <u>Geometric Progression:</u> A sequence of the form: $a+r, a+2r, a+3r, a+4r,$ etc. where $a$ and $r$ (the common ratio) are real numbers
- Given the first few terms of a sequence, our goal is to find a pattern that arises and a formula to describe the sequence
- <u>Ulam Numbers:</u> a sequence defined by first setting $a_1 = 1$ and  $a_2 = 2$.  
  The term $a_i$ for $i$ $> 2$ is defined to be the smallest integer greater than $a_i−1$ that can be uniquely written as the sum of two different Ulam numbers
- <u>Recursive Definitions:</u> Sequences can be defined using one or more initial terms (known as the basis), and then give a rule for determining any element $a_n$ using previous terms in the sequence
